TAJ MAHAL

The Taj Mahal is a breathtaking ivory-white marble mausoleum located on the southern bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, India. Commissioned in 1631 by the Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan to house the tomb of his favorite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, this UNESCO World Heritage Site stands as one of the world's most universally admired masterpieces of human heritage. The architecture seamlessly blends Indian, Persian, and Islamic design elements, achieving near-perfect symmetry across its vast 17-hectare complex. Its central white dome is flanked by four elegant minarets, surrounded by geometric reflecting pools and gardens. The structure is renowned for its intricate craftsmanship, featuring pietra dura inlays of semiprecious stones like lapis lazuli and jade, as well as calligraphic Quranic inscriptions. Remarkably, the translucent white Makrana marble shifts colors throughout the day, glowing pink in the soft morning light, brilliant white under the midday sun, and golden under the moon.
