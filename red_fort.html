RED FORT

The Red Fort (Lal Qila) is a historic 17th-century fortress built of red sandstone, located in the heart of Old Delhi, India. Commissioned by the Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan in 1638 when he shifted his capital from Agra to Delhi, this UNESCO World Heritage Site served as the main residence of the Mughal emperors for nearly 200 years. The fort's massive, octagonal red sandstone walls stretch for over two kilometers and enclose a brilliant fusion of Persian, Timurid, and Hindu architectural styles. Inside, the complex houses iconic structures like the Diwan-i-Aam (Hall of Public Audience), the exquisite Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience), and the beautiful Moti Masjid (Pearl Mosque). Beyond its architectural brilliance, the Red Fort holds immense political and symbolic importance for India; it is the site where the national flag was raised when the country gained independence in 1947, and where the Prime Minister continues to deliver an annual address every Independence Day.
